Issue 01

Lower Costs & Good Jobs

Working families are being squeezed from every direction. Consumer prices rose 3.7% over the past year as of September 2026, with gasoline alone up 9.1% in a single month, while the economy added just 29,000 jobs that month and unemployment climbed to 4.2%. Graham grew up in a house where a jump in the price of gas meant skipping something else, and he knows those numbers aren't abstract. As president, he will raise the $2,200 child tax credit to $3,000 for children under six, make the temporary federal tax deduction for overtime pay permanent for hourly workers, and cut the red tape that adds months and dollars to building new homes. He will also create a Made in America Manufacturing Credit for companies that build new plants in communities that have lost factory jobs. As Governor of Ohio, Graham balanced every budget without raising income taxes, and he'll bring that discipline to Washington.

Graham's pledge: A paycheck that goes further and a good job you don't have to move away to find.

Issue 02

Steel, Fair Trade & the National Debt

Graham was five when Youngstown lost its mills, and he built his own steel business from nothing, so he knows what fair trade means for towns like his. The 50% Section 232 tariffs on imported steel and aluminum, which took effect June 4, 2025, remain in place after the Supreme Court struck down the separate emergency tariffs in Learning Resources v. Trump on February 20, 2026, and Graham will keep them in force against countries that dump subsidized steel. He will work with Congress to put trade policy on solid legal footing so American manufacturers can plan for the long term instead of guessing month to month. At the same time, Washington's spending is out of control: the national debt passed $40 trillion in October 2026, and net interest cost $1.05 trillion in fiscal year 2026, more than the $956 billion spent on national defense. Graham will send Congress a balanced budget plan in his first year and use the Government Accountability Office's annual reports on duplicate programs to cut waste before asking taxpayers for another dime.

Graham's pledge: Protect American steel, and stop spending money our grandkids will have to pay back.

Issue 03

Affordable Energy & a Clean Environment

Ohio families paid 10.4% more per kilowatt-hour for electricity in August 2026 than a year earlier, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration, and a big reason is the rush of new data centers onto the regional grid. In the PJM grid that serves 13 states and Washington, D.C., capacity prices jumped from $28.92 per megawatt-day in 2024/25 to $329.17 in 2026/27, and the grid's independent market monitor found that data center demand alone added about $9.3 billion to the cost of the 2025/2026 auction. A Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory report found data centers used 4.4% of America's electricity in 2023 and could use between 6.7% and 12% by 2028. Graham supports American leadership in artificial intelligence, but he believes the tech companies building these facilities should pay for the new power plants and transmission lines they need, not grandmothers in Youngstown. He will speed up permits for natural gas from the Utica and Marcellus shale, back new small modular nuclear reactors, and fund the cleanup of old industrial sites so they can host new plants instead of sitting empty.

Graham's pledge: Big Tech pays its own power bill, and your bill goes down.

Issue 04

Health Care You Can Afford

When the enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025, families who kept the same plan saw their share of premiums more than double, rising 114% on average, and nearly three million people dropped their coverage. Many of them are self-employed contractors, farmers, and small business owners, the same people Graham worked alongside for decades. As president, he will push for a targeted bridge tax credit for middle-income families and require hospitals and insurers to post real prices up front so patients can shop for care. Graham will also protect rural hospitals, which serve communities where the nearest emergency room can be an hour away. He knows the cost of addiction firsthand from his time as governor, and he is encouraged that overdose deaths fell to about 70,000 in 2025, down from 81,313 in 2024, according to the CDC. He will expand the treatment and recovery programs that helped drive that progress.

Graham's pledge: Lower premiums, honest prices, and a hospital close to home.

Issue 05

Border Security & Legal Immigration

A secure border is the foundation of a fair immigration system. In fiscal year 2025, Border Patrol made 237,565 apprehensions at the Southwest border, the lowest number since 1970, which proves that enforcement works when Washington has the will to do it. Graham will keep those gains by fully funding Border Patrol, finishing barrier construction where agents say it's needed, and deploying modern sensors and drones along the entire border. In February 2025 the State Department designated the Sinaloa Cartel and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel as Foreign Terrorist Organizations, and Graham will use every financial and law enforcement tool that designation allows to cut off the fentanyl pipeline. He also believes legal immigration should serve American workers, which means a visa system based on real labor shortages that employers can prove, with mandatory E-Verify so no company can undercut wages by hiring illegally.

Graham's pledge: A secure border, a legal system that works, and no more cartel fentanyl in our towns.

Issue 06

National Defense, Foreign Policy & Veterans

Graham served in Operation Desert Storm, and he believes the best way to prevent war is to be so strong that no one wants to start one. He will strengthen America's position in the Western Hemisphere, starting with the Panama Canal, which the United States handed to Panama on December 31, 1999, under the 1977 Torrijos-Carter Treaties and which carries roughly 40% of U.S. container traffic. Graham supported Panama's decision in February 2026 to void the port contracts held by Hong Kong-based CK Hutchison, and he will work with Panama to keep Chinese influence away from the canal. He also backs the new U.S.-Denmark-Greenland security agreement, which expands America's military presence at places like Pituffik Space Base. The United States pays 22% of the United Nations' regular budget, so Graham will tie future payments to real reforms and audits. He supports U.S.-brokered talks to end the war in Ukraine with strong security guarantees so Russia cannot invade again.

For veterans, Graham will fully fund the PACT Act, the 2022 law that expanded health care and benefits for veterans exposed to burn pits and other toxic substances. He will also guarantee community care for any veteran who can't get a VA appointment in a reasonable time.

Graham's pledge: Peace through strength, and every promise to our veterans kept.

Issue 07

Public Safety & the Second Amendment

Graham is a gun owner, a hunter, and an Army veteran who believes the Second Amendment protects an individual right. The Supreme Court confirmed in New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen on June 23, 2022, that the right to bear arms extends outside the home, and Graham will appoint judges who respect that decision. Ohio's permitless carry law took effect on June 13, 2022, allowing law-abiding adults 21 and older to carry without a permit, and Graham believes responsible gun owners in every state should be treated with the same respect. He also knows that real public safety means supporting the police: according to the FBI, violent crime fell 4.5% and murder fell an estimated 14.9% in 2024, and Graham wants to keep that progress going. As president, he will expand federal grants for hiring and training local officers, go after repeat violent offenders and illegal gun traffickers, and invest in mental health crisis teams that work alongside police.

Graham's pledge: Protect your rights, back the blue, and keep violent criminals off our streets.

Issue 08

Strong Families & Communities

Too many young Americans say they can't afford to start a family. The CDC reported in October 2026 that the U.S. general fertility rate fell to a record low of 53.2 births per 1,000 women in 2025, with 3,612,012 babies born. Graham and Brittany raised four kids on a small business income, and they know how much child care, housing, and health insurance can strain a young family. As president, Graham will expand the child care tax credit, encourage employers to offer paid family leave through a new small business tax credit, and remove federal barriers that keep states from building more affordable starter homes. He will also expand apprenticeships and skilled trades training in high schools so students have a path to a good job without a mountain of college debt. Graham believes every parent should have a say in their child's education, and he supports school choice so a family's ZIP code never decides their child's future.

Graham's pledge: Make it possible again to raise a family on one good paycheck.

Issue 09

American Frontier

Operation Red Burrow

At the first Democratic primary debate, Gov. Joe Pemberton said Graham "belongs on Mars with the rest of the mole rats," and Graham thinks the mole rats deserve better than a punchline. Naked mole rats can survive about 18 minutes with no oxygen at all by switching their brains to burning fructose, a discovery published in Science in 2017. Researchers at the University of Rochester have spent years studying why these animals almost never develop cancer and live more than 30 years, longer than any other rodent. Those are exactly the problems astronauts will face on Mars, where the thin air is about 95% carbon dioxide and long missions expose crews to radiation that raises cancer risk. Operation Red Burrow would direct NASA to fly a small colony of naked mole rats on an early Mars cargo mission, building on a long record of animal research in space that runs from Laika in 1957 and Ham the chimpanzee in 1961 to NASA's Rodent Research missions aboard the International Space Station. Because Earth and Mars line up for an efficient trip only about once every 26 months, Graham will ask NASA to target the first launch window of his term. He admits it will also prove Joe wrong.

Frontier Builders

NASA's Artemis II crew flew around the Moon in April 2026, the first humans to travel there since 1972, and NASA plans to land astronauts near the lunar South Pole on Artemis IV in 2028. Landing is only the beginning, because a permanent base needs shelters that crews can build, repair, and keep running far from any spare-parts supply. Graham's Frontier Builders program would invite volunteer Amish and Mennonite craftsmen, whose communities have built durable homes and barns with simple, repairable methods for generations, to help NASA engineers design and build prototype habitats here on Earth. At a traditional Amish barn raising, hundreds of neighbors can frame and roof an entire barn in a single day, and that kind of coordination and craftsmanship is exactly what low-tech space construction needs. The Amish population in North America reached about 421,200 in 2026, up 32% since 2017, with nearly 60% living in Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Indiana, so the program would partner with builders close to home. Participation would be strictly voluntary and shaped around each community's own practices. Heavy-lift rockets like Starship, which reached orbit for the first time on September 28, 2026, are expected to carry the finished designs to the Moon and Mars. NASA plans to retire the International Space Station around 2030 using an $843 million SpaceX deorbit vehicle, and Graham wants American-built habitats ready on the Moon before it comes down.

Graham's pledge: Americans first on Mars, living in habitats built by American hands.
