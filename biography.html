Meet Graham

A son of the Rust Belt. A soldier, a builder, and a governor. A fighter for every family Washington forgot.

GRAHAM L. JANICIK
His Story

Where Graham comes from

Graham Lewis Janicik was born in 1972 on the east side of Youngstown, Ohio, a steel town that had already begun to lose its mills. He was five years old on September 19, 1977, the day Youngstown Sheet & Tube announced it would close its Campbell Works and lay off 5,000 workers, a day the Mahoning Valley still calls Black Monday. One of those workers was his dad Dale, who later found work driving a truck, while his other dad, Marcus, worked nights as a nurse's aide. There were stretches when the family relied on food stamps and the church pantry to get by, and Graham started mowing lawns at twelve to help with the electric bill. His dads taught him to work hard, keep his word, and take care of his neighbors, lessons he has carried ever since.

At eighteen, Graham enlisted in the U.S. Army. He served as a combat engineer with VII Corps during Operation Desert Storm in 1991, clearing paths for American tanks through the Iraqi desert. When he came home, he used the GI Bill to earn an engineering technology degree from Youngstown State University, the first in his family to finish college. In 1999 he borrowed against his truck to open Janicik Fabrication, a two-man steel shop in a vacant Campbell warehouse. Over the next fifteen years he grew it into a company with 300 union employees, making structural steel for bridges, hospitals, and schools across the Midwest. Along the way he married Brittany, a Youngstown school nurse, and together they raised four children.

Graham never planned to run for office, but after watching too many neighbors leave Ohio to find work, he decided someone had to fight for the people staying behind. As a two-term Governor of Ohio, he balanced every budget without raising income taxes, expanded addiction treatment in all 88 counties during the worst of the opioid crisis, and recruited new manufacturing and energy jobs to communities that had been written off for decades. He brought the same habits he learned in the Army and on the shop floor: show up early, listen to the people doing the work, and never ask anyone to do something you wouldn't do yourself. Republicans, Democrats, and independents alike sent him back to Columbus for a second term.

Graham believes America's best days are still ahead of it. He believes that a kid growing up in a small town, raised by two dads or a single mom or grandparents, should have the same shot at a good life as anyone else. He believes parents should be able to buy a home, fill up the tank, and save for retirement on one good paycheck. He believes the men and women who serve this country deserve a government that keeps its promises to them. And he believes Americans have far more in common than the politicians in Washington want them to think. Graham is running for president to bring that common sense back to the White House.

    Graduates high school and enlists in the U.S. Army.

    Serves as a combat engineer with VII Corps in Operation Desert Storm.

The Family

Faith, family, and the Mahoning Valley

Graham and Brittany have been married for 30 years and still live in the same Boardman neighborhood where they raised their four children: Emily, a Navy nurse; Ryan, a union electrician; Jackson, a high school teacher; and Grace, a senior at Ohio State. They are also proud grandparents of two. On Sundays you'll find Graham at St. Columba Cathedral and then grilling with his dads, and he hasn't missed a Youngstown State Penguins home opener in 25 years.

"I'm not running because I think I'm better than anybody. I'm running because I know exactly who I'm fighting for." Graham, announcement speech
