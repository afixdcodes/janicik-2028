STEEL COUNTRY COMEBACK: U.S. Steel Bets $3 Billion on the Mon Valley as Tariffs Pay Off for American Workers

October 23, 2026

U.S. Steel announced on October 22 that it will invest about $3 billion in new hot-rolling equipment at its Edgar Thomson plant in Pennsylvania's Mon Valley. The project is part of the $11 billion that owner Nippon Steel has committed to U.S. operations by 2028, under a deal that gives the federal government a "golden share" to block plant closures or moves overseas. The 50% Section 232 steel tariffs remain in place even after the Supreme Court struck down separate emergency tariffs in February. "This is what happens when you stand up for American steel," said Janicik, who grew up in the shadow of Youngstown's closed mills. "Joe Pemberton has never once set foot in a steel mill. I built a steel company."

LIGHTS OUT FOR FAMILIES: Ohio Power Bills Up More Than 10% as Big Tech Data Centers Gobble Up the Grid

October 14, 2026

Residential electricity prices in August 2026 were up 10.4% in Ohio and 9.2% in Pennsylvania from a year earlier, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. In the PJM grid that serves both states, capacity prices have soared from $28.92 per megawatt-day in 2024/25 to $329.17 in 2026/27, and the grid's independent market monitor found that data center demand alone added about $9.3 billion to the cost of the 2025/2026 auction. Pemberton's answer is new subsidies for wind and solar projects that take years to connect to the grid. Janicik's plan would require big data center developers to pay for the power plants and transmission lines they need. "Silicon Valley can afford its own power bill," Janicik told a crowd in Columbus. "Grandma in Youngstown shouldn't have to pay for it."

CANAL VICTORY: Panama Kicks Out Chinese-Linked Port Operator, Hands Terminals to Western Firms

October 2, 2026

Panama formally voided Hong Kong-based CK Hutchison's contracts to run the ports at both ends of the Panama Canal in February 2026 and handed interim control to Maersk and MSC. CK Hutchison has since launched international arbitration seeking more than $1.5 billion. The canal carries roughly 40% of all U.S. container traffic, and America built it and ran it until handing it over on December 31, 1999, under the 1977 Torrijos-Carter Treaties. "Our Navy and our shippers depend on that canal," said Janicik, a Desert Storm veteran. "As president, I'll make sure China never gets a foothold there again."
