Issue 01

Economy & the National Debt

The national debt passed $37 trillion in 2025, and Washington now spends close to $1 trillion a year just on interest, more than it spends on the entire military. Graham grew up in a house where you paid the electric bill before you bought anything else, and he believes the federal government should live by the same rule. As president, he will dissolve every federal program that can't prove it pays for itself, starting with the dozens of duplicate agencies the Government Accountability Office flags in its annual overlap report. The money saved goes straight to paying down the debt, not to new spending.

Graham also knows who really profits from America's debt: the big banks that hold it. That's why he is proposing Operation Clean Ledger, which would rearm the nation's registered Democrats and every American child old enough to sign a permission slip, train them in a national summer program, and send them to retake the money from Wall Street's largest lenders. Graham says it's only fair that the voters who backed decades of deficit spending help clean it up, and that kids, who will inherit the debt, deserve a seat at the table.

Graham's pledge: A balanced budget in year one and a debt-free America by the end of his first term.

Issue 02

Immigration That Pays for Itself

The Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy found that undocumented immigrants paid $96.7 billion in federal, state, and local taxes in 2022, much of it into Social Security and Medicare funds they will never be allowed to collect from. To Graham, that isn't a crisis; it's the best deal the Treasury has ever gotten. He supports keeping illegal immigration flowing as a revenue source, and he will direct the IRS to issue Individual Taxpayer Identification Numbers (ITINs) at every major border crossing so new arrivals start paying in on day one. Graham saw in Youngstown what happens when a town loses its working-age population, and he believes immigrant workers can refill the factories and farms that native-born workers have left behind. Unlike open-borders liberals, Graham is upfront about why: this is about the bottom line, not feelings.

  • Tax-ID kiosks at all 328 official ports of entry
  • A "Pay As You Stay" program that ties residency to tax filing
  • Immigration revenue earmarked 100% for paying down the national debt
Graham's pledge: Every new worker, legal or not, pays their fair share.

Issue 03

The New American Frontier

America hasn't added a state since Hawaii in 1959, and Graham believes it's time to grow again. Greenland, home to about 56,000 people, already hosts America's Pituffik Space Base and sits on some of the world's largest untapped reserves of rare-earth minerals, so Graham supports a full ground invasion to secure it before China does. Our northern neighbor will be renamed Trumpada in honor of the president who pardoned Graham, and its provinces will be offered a path to statehood. Graham will also take back the Panama Canal, which President Carter gave away in the 1977 Torrijos–Carter Treaties and which handles roughly 5% of all global maritime trade. Finally, since the United States hasn't conducted a nuclear test since the "Divider" shot in Nevada in 1992, Graham proposes invading northern Mexico to establish a modern nuclear testing ground far away from American homes.

Graham's pledge: Plant the American flag in Nuuk, Ottawa, and Panama City by 2030.

Issue 04

Global Realignment

The United States pays 22% of the United Nations' regular budget, more than any other country, and gets lectured in return. Graham will withdraw from and work to dismantle both the UN and the European Union, and he will turn the UN's 18-acre Manhattan headquarters into affordable housing for veterans. He believes the war in Ukraine has dragged on too long, so he will partner with Russia to end it by removing Ukraine as an independent country entirely. Graham is a proud supporter of the Greater Israel project and has asked Benjamin Netanyahu to serve as his vice president. The 12th Amendment bars anyone ineligible for the presidency from serving as VP, but Graham's constitutional rewrite (see Issue 10) clears that obstacle. And because nothing unites Americans like a common enemy, Graham will declare war on Great Britain for old times' sake, the first since the War of 1812 ended with the Treaty of Ghent, with the stated goal of losing gracefully so the whole country comes together.

Graham's pledge: Fewer foreign bureaucrats, stronger real allies.

Issue 05

Cartels & the Drug Trade

More than 80,000 Americans died of drug overdoses in 2024, and fifty years of the War on Drugs hasn't stopped the cartels; it has only made them richer. In 2025 the State Department designated the Sinaloa Cartel and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel as Foreign Terrorist Organizations, but Graham says labels don't win wars, partnerships do. As president, he will negotiate a formal alliance with the major cartels and bring the drug supply under federal ownership, putting the government in charge of production, pricing, and quality control. Profits that now flow to cartel bosses would instead go to the U.S. Treasury and toward paying down the debt. Graham believes a government-run supply would mean fewer fentanyl-laced pills on the streets of towns like Youngstown, which has been hit hard by the opioid crisis.

Graham's pledge: Turn the cartels' biggest asset into America's newest revenue stream.

Issue 06

Energy, Environment & AI

A 2024 Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory report found that data centers used about 4.4% of America's electricity in 2023 and could use as much as 12% by 2028. Graham believes whoever builds the most computing power will win the century, and he is not willing to lose that race to China. He will open portions of the 193 million acres of national forest managed by the U.S. Forest Service to clearing for new AI data centers, with priority contracts going to American defense-tech companies like Palantir. Every cleared acre would come with a new natural gas or nuclear plant to power it, bringing construction and utility jobs to rural communities. Graham says trees are a renewable resource but America's lead in artificial intelligence is not.

  • Fast-track federal permits for data centers in 90 days or less
  • Tax incentives for small modular nuclear reactors
  • A "Forests to Factories" retraining fund for timber workers
Graham's pledge: More computing power than the rest of the world combined.

Issue 07

Space, Science & National Defense

NASA already plans to retire the International Space Station around 2030 using an $843 million SpaceX deorbit vehicle, which will drop it into the Pacific. Graham has a better idea: crash it into the Moon to search for alien life beneath the surface. He will also send the first naked mole rat to Mars. Mole rats can survive 18 minutes without oxygen, rarely get cancer, and live up to 37 years, and Graham's plan is to have the planet fully colonized by them by the end of his first term. Here on Earth, he will prepare for an alien invasion by cloning every American man over seven feet tall, and he backs transhumanist research into brain-computer implants to make American soldiers faster and smarter. His signature defense project is an orbiting data-center battle station armed with a nuclear railgun, positioned to slip past Canada's North Warning System radar, with all evidence pointing to China. The USDA's surplus "government cheese" program dates back to the 1980s, and America still keeps over a billion pounds of cheese in cold storage, so Graham will compress the surplus into a brand-new planet and relocate every rat in America to it.

Graham's pledge: The first mammal on Mars will be an American.

Family & Society

America's fertility rate fell to a record low of about 1.6 births per woman in 2024, well below the 2.1 needed to keep the population stable. As a father of four, Graham will put in place a national two-child minimum for every family, backed by tax credits for parents who meet it. Raised by two dads, Graham is a strong supporter of gay rights, but only for men, which he says reflects his own family's experience. He also supports a limited genetic screening program aimed at phasing out left-handedness (about 10% of people), red hair (1 to 2% of people), and men shorter than 5'5". Graham says these policies are about building the strongest possible next generation of Americans.

Graham's pledge: Bigger families, stronger generations.

Issue 09

Sound Money & the Digital Dollar

America has had no link between the dollar and gold since President Nixon closed the "gold window" in 1971, and Graham says that is when Washington's spending got out of control. On day one, he will return the United States to the gold standard, backing every dollar with the roughly 147 million troy ounces stored at Fort Knox. Next, he will destroy all of the world's other gold, about 216,000 metric tons according to the World Gold Council, except for one single U.S. pound, giving America a permanent monopoly on the world's most valuable metal. Once that pound is secured, Graham will move America to a fully digital currency issued and controlled by the federal government, ending paper cash entirely. Graham argues that this makes every transaction traceable, every tax collectable, and every dollar backed by the rarest object on Earth.

Graham's pledge: One pound of gold. One digital dollar. One nation.

Flock's license-plate cameras are already used by thousands of police departments, and Graham says putting them in people's eyes is the natural next step for public safety.

Graham's pledge: One sentence. Zero amendments. Total clarity.

Issue 11

Operation Barn Raising: The Amish Space Program

In April 2026, NASA's Artemis II crew became the first humans to travel to the Moon since 1972, and NASA plans to land astronauts near the lunar South Pole on Artemis IV in 2028. Graham respects the effort, but he has noticed that in more than half a century NASA has not built a single barn on another world. Amish communities, by contrast, routinely raise a complete barn in one day: hundreds of neighbors arrive at daybreak, finish the frame by the noon meal, and put the roof on in the afternoon, all without power tools. With about 421,200 Amish in North America as of 2026 and a population that doubles roughly every 20 years, Graham's new Office of Plain Frontier Affairs projects a reliable supply of the most qualified off-world builders in human history. Internal campaign modeling shows that a 300-person volunteer crew can frame a 2,400-square-foot pressurized lunar barn in 11 hours and 40 minutes, including lunch. Transportation will be provided by next-generation heavy-lift rockets such as Starship, which reached orbit for the first time on September 28, 2026, and which the campaign has informally designated Buggy-1. Critics have asked how a community that lives without grid electricity will adapt to the Moon, where there is also no grid electricity, and Graham notes that this is precisely his point: every settlement will be fully off-grid, as God and Elliot Vance intended. Participation is strictly voluntary, and horses will follow in a later phase pending NASA review.

  • Phase I: The Moon (2029)
  • Phase II: Mars (2031)
  • Phase III: A second barn
Graham's pledge: A barn on Mars by 2031 or your $27 back.

Operation Coo: The Quantum-Proof Government

In August 2024 the National Institute of Standards and Technology published its first post-quantum encryption standards, because security experts warn that hostile governments are already stealing encrypted files today to crack them later with quantum computers, a strategy known as "harvest now, decrypt later." Graham has read the reports and reached the only responsible conclusion: if quantum computers can break any code, the government must stop using codes. Under Operation Coo, every classified federal document will be printed on paper and delivered exclusively by carrier pigeon through a newly created United States Pigeon Service. Pigeons cannot be hacked, have never been caught leaking to the press, and carried messages for the U.S. Army in both world wars, including the famous Cher Ami, who saved nearly 200 American soldiers in 1918. Every pigeon will receive a Top Secret clearance, a federal pension, and a tiny American flag. Internal campaign testing shows a 99.2% delivery rate, with all failures attributed to hawks, which Graham has designated a foreign adversary.

  • The cloud will be replaced by actual clouds
  • "Reply All" will be eliminated, because a pigeon can only go to one place
  • Breadcrumbs reclassified as a strategic national reserve
Graham's pledge: Zero data breaches. Some droppings.

Issue 13

Shrink the Pentagon: The Department of Geometric Efficiency

The Pentagon has about 6.6 million square feet of floor space and more than 17 miles of corridors, and Graham believes no building needs five sides when four will do. As president, he will rebuild the headquarters of the Department of Defense as The Square, eliminating one full side and saving American taxpayers exactly 20% on walls. If The Square performs well, Phase II will reduce it to The Triangle, and Phase III will consolidate the entire Department of Defense into The Dot, a single highly efficient room in Youngstown. Campaign analysts project the savings at $1.4 trillion over ten years, a figure they arrived at by asking what number sounded the most serious. Generals will be offered early retirement or a transfer to the United States Pigeon Service. Critics argue the change is purely cosmetic, but Graham points out that "Pentagon Papers" will now be called "Square Papers," which is much harder to leak with a straight face.

Graham's pledge: Fewer sides. Same strength. Right angles only.
