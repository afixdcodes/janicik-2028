Meet Graham

A son of the Rust Belt. A father of four. A fighter for every family Washington forgot.

GRAHAM L. JANICIK
His Story

Where Graham comes from

Graham Lewis Janicik was born in 1999 on the east side of Youngstown, Ohio, a city that lost more than half its population after the steel mills closed. He was raised by his two dads, Dale, a long-haul trucker, and Marcus, a night-shift nurse's aide, in a two-bedroom rental where the heat got shut off more than one winter. The family relied on SNAP benefits and church food pantries to get by, and by twelve Graham was mowing lawns and shoveling driveways to help cover the electric bill. His dads never had much money, but they taught him to work harder than the next guy, to look people in the eye, and to never let anyone tell him where he belonged.

Graham didn't wait for a scholarship or a connection to open doors for him. At sixteen he was flipping used cars out of a neighbor's driveway, and at nineteen, with no college degree and no family money, he founded his own investment company out of a Youngstown coffee shop. Within a few years he had turned it into a multimillion-dollar business, and he has always said he learned more about money from his first failed deals than any Ivy League professor could have taught him. That same year he married his high-school sweetheart, Brittany, a former 4-H champion and pediatric dental hygienist. Today they are raising their four children, Liberty, Ronald, Jackson, and baby Grace, in the same Mahoning Valley that raised him.

Graham believes his story is what America is supposed to look like: a kid from a struggling family who outworked everyone around him. Running a company taught him how to balance a budget, meet a payroll, and make hard calls quickly, and that's the experience he wants to bring to a government that hasn't balanced its own books in a generation. He has also spent time as a defendant on the wrong end of the federal justice system, an experience he says gave him firsthand understanding of how Washington treats ordinary people who don't have the right last name. At twenty-seven, he would be the youngest person ever elected to the presidency, and he sees that as an asset: the people making decisions should be the ones who will have to live with them.

Graham's vision for America is simple. Every kid, whether they're raised in a mansion or a trailer, by a mom and a dad or by two dads, should have a real shot at building the life they want. Families should be able to afford groceries, gas, and a home without drowning in debt. Our government should spend within its means, keep its promises, and stop treating hardworking people like an afterthought. And America should go back to dreaming big, whether that means new industries at home or new frontiers in space. Graham isn't running to be part of the system. He's running because he grew up outside of it, and he knows how many Americans are still out there waiting for someone to fight for them.

The Journey

From Youngstown to the White House

  • 1999
    Born in Youngstown, Ohio, to Dale and Marcus Janicik.
  • 2011
    Starts his first business mowing lawns at age 12 to help pay the family's bills.
  • 2015
    Begins buying, fixing, and reselling used cars out of a neighbor's driveway.
  • 2018
    Founds Janicik Capital Partners and marries his high-school sweetheart, Brittany.
  • 2019–2025
    Welcomes four children: Liberty, Ronald, Jackson, and Grace.
  • 2026
    Announces his run for President of the United States in front of the shuttered Youngstown Sheet & Tube site.
The Family

Faith, family, and the Mahoning Valley

When he isn't on the campaign trail, Graham coaches his oldest daughter's youth wrestling team, grills on Sundays with his dads, and has never missed a Youngstown State Penguins home opener. Brittany leads the campaign's "Moms for Graham" outreach and has visited more than 40 rural hospitals and clinics across Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Michigan.

"I'm not running because I think I'm better than anybody. I'm running because I know exactly who I'm fighting for." Graham, announcement speech
